# Physics Figures

Physics Figures (physicsfigures.com) is a directory of generators that make
clean, printable physics figures for teachers to paste into tests, worksheets
and slides. It is a teacher.dev project.

## Language

### The directory

**Figure**:
A finished physics picture a teacher puts in front of students, such as a coil with a moving magnet or a block on an inclined plane.
_Avoid_: Graphic, image, visual, resource, diagram (except inside a figure's standard name, such as a free body diagram or circuit diagram, where it stays in the generator's name and search words)

**Generator**:
A page that makes one kind of figure from a teacher's settings, named "<Figure> Generator".
_Avoid_: Maker, tool, builder, app

**Directory**:
The home page, which lists every generator with a live preview of its figure. There are no generators yet, so it shows only the card for a generator request.
_Avoid_: Catalog, gallery, index

**Generator request**:
A teacher asking for a kind of figure that no generator makes yet.
_Avoid_: Feature request, suggestion

### Parts of every figure

**Object**:
A block, ball, cart or hanging mass in a figure. Its drawn size is its own setting, apart from its label, and every object starts the same size, so a 5 kg block isn't bigger than a 2 kg one unless the teacher makes it so.
_Avoid_: Mass (for the thing drawn; fine inside a label), body, item

**Mirror**:
A generator's one switch that flips its figure left to right, so a ramp rises the other way or a magnet comes from the other side. Letters and numbers stay readable, and N and S keep their meaning.
_Avoid_: Flip, reverse, direction

**Label**:
Text drawn beside a part of a figure, such as m₁, 5 kg, 30°, θ, N or S. Each one can be written text, a blank line for students to fill in, or left off.
_Avoid_: Caption, tag, annotation

**Vector**:
An arrow drawn on a figure for a force, velocity or acceleration, such as gravity on a block or the motion of a magnet. It is drawn about the right size, not to scale, looks the same in every generator, and has its own label. Forces are solid; velocity and acceleration have a dashed shaft at full weight, so they're never read as forces, even photocopied. On a Free Body Diagram the teacher sets each force's length relative to the others, so equal forces are drawn equal. On a Vector Diagram vectors are drawn to scale, and the teacher picks solid or dashed for each one.
_Avoid_: Arrow (alone), force line

**Chart title**:
The title across the top of a figure.
_Avoid_: Title (alone), heading

**Preset**:
A named set of settings for one generator, saved by the teacher in their browser. None are built in.
_Avoid_: Template, favorite

### Coil and Magnet

**Coil and Magnet**:
A figure of a coil of wire seen from the side, with a bar magnet, a battery or nothing beside it, for electromagnetic induction and electromagnets. Its generator is the Coil and Magnet Generator.
_Avoid_: Induction diagram, solenoid (fine as a search word)

**Field line**:
A curve showing the direction of a magnetic or electric field, with arrowheads along it. Field lines follow the real shape of the field, so they never cross. The Electric and Magnetic Field figure uses the word too.
_Avoid_: Flux line, field arrow

**Meter**:
The galvanometer that can be wired to a coil, with its needle to the left, right, centered or blank.
_Avoid_: Ammeter, gauge

**Current arrow**:
A small arrow on a coil's wire showing which way the current flows. It often gives away the answer, so it can be turned off.
_Avoid_: Current direction, flow arrow

### Electric and Magnetic Field

**Electric and Magnetic Field**:
A figure of the field lines around one of six setups: one point charge, opposite charges, like charges, parallel plates, a bar magnet, or a wire seen end-on. Its generator is the Electric and Magnetic Field Generator.
_Avoid_: Field diagram (fine as a search word), E-field, B-field

**Setup**:
Which of the six things the Electric and Magnetic Field figure draws the field of. Charges are always equal in size; one sign switch makes a single charge, or both like charges, positive or negative.
_Avoid_: Configuration, scenario, arrangement

**Point charge**:
A charge drawn as a circle with + or − in it. Its field lines start evenly spaced around it.
_Avoid_: Particle, ion

**Wire, end-on**:
A straight wire carrying a current, seen from one end: a circle with a dot when the current flows out of the page, a cross when it flows into it. Its field lines are circles around it.
_Avoid_: Conductor, current element

### Inclined Plane

**Inclined Plane**:
A figure of a ramp at an angle with an object on it, a block, a ball or a cart, or a row of up to three. Its generator is the Inclined Plane Generator. A ramp with a pulley on it belongs to the Pulley.
_Avoid_: Ramp (fine as a search word), slope, wedge

**Row**:
Two or three objects side by side on a slope, table or ramp, tied by strings or touching. Each one has its own gravity, normal force and friction, numbered from the back (or the foot) on. Objects touching push on each other with a pair of contact forces, move as one, and get one velocity or acceleration arrow.
_Avoid_: Train, chain, system (fine as a search word)

### Pulley

**Pulley**:
A figure of objects connected by string over one or more pulleys, in one of its setups. Its generator is the Pulley Generator. Every figure with a pulley is a Pulley figure; objects tied in a row on a slope with no pulley are an Inclined Plane.
_Avoid_: Pulley system, rope diagram

**Setup**:
One arrangement a Pulley figure can take: Atwood machine (two hanging objects over one fixed pulley, and maybe a third hanging below one of them), table and hanging mass, ramp and hanging mass (each with one object, or a row of two, on the table or ramp), or block and tackle (one load held by 1 to 4 strands).
_Avoid_: Mode, type, scenario

**String**:
One piece of string between two objects, over any pulleys between them. When a figure has more than one, each has its own tension, numbered T₁, T₂.
_Avoid_: Rope (fine in a block and tackle), cord, segment

### Free Body Diagram

**Free Body Diagram**:
A figure of one body alone, a dot or an Object, with every force on it drawn from its middle and nothing around it: no ramp, string or floor. Its generator is the Free Body Diagram Generator. The ramp or pulley a problem is about is an Inclined Plane or Pulley figure; its free body diagram is this one.
_Avoid_: Force diagram (fine as a search word), FBD (fine as a search word)

**Body**:
What a Free Body Diagram is of: a dot (the usual AP convention) or a block, ball or cart. It has no label.
_Avoid_: Particle, point mass

**Force list**:
The forces on a Free Body Diagram, up to eight, each with its own angle (counterclockwise from the right), length relative to the others, and label. Starter buttons add the usual ones (gravity, normal, friction, tension, applied, spring, air resistance) pointing the usual way.
_Avoid_: Force slots

**Angle mark**:
An arc on a Free Body Diagram between an angled force and a dashed horizontal or vertical reference line, with its own label, measured from the nearer half of that line.
_Avoid_: Angle arc (alone)

**Component**:
One of the two thinner, dashed, lighter arrows along the horizontal and vertical that add up to an angled force. Components often give away the answer, so each force's are off unless turned on.
_Avoid_: Projection, part

### Projectile Motion

**Projectile Motion**:
A figure of a ball or dot launched to the right from level ground or off the top of a cliff, with the path it follows until it lands on the ground. The ground is a plain line, like an axis, not a hatched floor, and on it the ball's middle sits on the line at the launch and the landing. Its generator is the Projectile Motion Generator. The path is the real parabola for the launch angle and cliff height, drawn to fit, while its vectors are drawn about the right size, not to scale.
_Avoid_: Trajectory diagram (fine as a search word), projectile (alone, for the figure), cannon

**Launch**:
Where the ball starts and how: from level ground at 10° to 85°, or from a cliff at 0° (a horizontal launch) to 85°, with the launch velocity, its angle mark and components drawn there. Air resistance is never drawn.
_Avoid_: Throw, kick, fire

**Path**:
The dashed curve the ball's middle follows from the launch to the ground. It can be hidden for "sketch the path" questions.
_Avoid_: Trajectory (fine as a search word), arc (that's an angle mark's), curve

**Time step**:
The ball drawn again along the path at equal times after the launch, the last one where it lands, so its even spacing across and uneven spacing down show. They can be lettered A, B, C… from the launch.
_Avoid_: Snapshot, frame, strobe (fine as a search word), point (that's a circuit's)

### Vector Diagram

**Vector Diagram**:
A figure of up to three vectors drawn to scale head to tail, each set by its magnitude and direction, with their resultant, for vector addition and components. Its generator is the Vector Diagram Generator. It has no body and says nothing about what the vectors are, so it serves displacement, velocity and force problems alike. Forces on one body drawn from its middle are a Free Body Diagram. There is no vector subtraction: a teacher who wants −B sets B's direction the other way.
_Avoid_: Vector addition diagram (fine as a search word), head-to-tail diagram

**Magnitude**:
How long a vector on a Vector Diagram is, in grid squares, from 0.5 to 12.
_Avoid_: Length, size

**Resultant**:
The vector from the first vector's tail to the last one's tip: the sum. It is drawn solid, dashed or left off like any other vector, and the generator shows its magnitude and direction for the answer key.
_Avoid_: Net vector, sum vector, total

**Arrow style**:
Whether a vector on a Vector Diagram is drawn solid, drawn dashed, or left off. A vector left off still takes its place in the chain, so the gap is there for students to draw it in.
_Avoid_: Line style, visibility

**Grid**:
Light squares behind a Vector Diagram, one per unit of magnitude, lined up with the first tail. It has no tick marks or numbers. On by default.
_Avoid_: Graph paper (fine as a search word)

**Axes**:
Plain x and y axes through the first tail of a Vector Diagram, with an arrowhead at each end and x and y at the positive ends, but no tick marks. Off by default.
_Avoid_: Coordinate system

On a Vector Diagram, an **angle mark** is drawn at the arrow's tail and a **component** pair runs head to tail from it (along the horizontal, then the vertical to the tip), for any vector or the resultant. Both are off by default.

### Wave

**Wave**:
A figure of a transverse wave, a longitudinal wave, or a longitudinal wave above the transverse wave it matches, for measuring wavelength, period and amplitude. Its generator is the Wave Generator. The transverse wave is the exact sine curve, starting on its rest line at 0 and rising.
_Avoid_: Wave diagram (fine as a search word), sine wave (fine as a search word), oscillation

**Rest line**:
The line a transverse wave swings about: the x-axis on numbered axes, or a dashed line without them. The amplitude is measured from it.
_Avoid_: Equilibrium line (fine in captions), center line, midline

**Longitudinal wave**:
A row of vertical lines, bunched at each **compression** and spread out at each **rarefaction**. Drawn with a transverse wave, its compressions sit over the crests.
_Avoid_: Slinky, density diagram

**Mark**:
On a Wave figure, an arrow with a head at each end measuring the wavelength (or the period, against time) or the amplitude, with its own label.
_Avoid_: Dimension, measurement, bracket
### Motion Graph

**Motion Graph**:
A figure of one motion along a line: its position–time, velocity–time or acceleration–time graph, or all three stacked on the same time axis. Its generator is the Motion Graph Generator. Every graph is worked out from the velocity–time graph, so they always match.
_Avoid_: Kinematics graph, x–t graph, distance–time graph (all fine as search words)

**Segment**:
One stretch of the motion, up to six in a row: at rest, constant velocity forward or back, speeding up or slowing down, with its speed (the one it moves at, or speeds up or slows down to) and how long it lasts typed in. It is a straight line on the velocity–time graph. Speeding up and slowing down carry on from the velocity before them, and slowing down stops at rest rather than turning around. The Motion Graph and the Motion Map share segments: the same editor, the same motion worked out from them, and the same page address, so each page opens the other's motion with a plain link. A change to segments changes both generators.
_Avoid_: Section, phase, interval

**Segment end**:
Where a segment starts or ends, lettered A, B, C… from the start, for questions like "what is the cart doing between B and C?". The letters are the same on every graph.
_Avoid_: Point (that's a circuit's), node

**Tangent**:
A dashed line touching the position–time graph at a chosen time. Its slope is the instantaneous velocity there.
_Avoid_: Slope line

**Ranges**:
Each axis's From, To and Count by. They are fitted to the motion unless the teacher sets them, to make several graphs on a worksheet match. Stacked graphs share the time axis's range; each has its own up its side.
_Avoid_: Scale, window, zoom

### Motion Map

**Motion Map**:
A figure of one motion along a line as a dot at equal times along a number line in meters, with a velocity arrow above each moving dot. Its generator is the Motion Map Generator. Its motion is built from the Motion Graph's segments and worked out by the same code, so a motion map and a motion graph from the same segments always show the same motion. Vertical motion, two objects on one map and motion on a plane are not drawn.
_Avoid_: Motion diagram, dot diagram, ticker tape, strobe diagram (all fine as search words)

**Dot**:
Where the object is at one of the equal times, 0.5 s, 1 s or 2 s apart (the time between dots, Δt). Dots at one spot while it's at rest are drawn as one dot, with the times it's there (t = 12–15 s) and no arrow. At a segment end a dot takes the later segment's velocity. A segment end that falls between dots has no dot, and the settings panel says so.
_Avoid_: Point (that's a circuit's), position, mark, time step (that's a Projectile Motion's)

**Run**:
The dots from where the motion starts or turns back to where it next turns back. Each run is drawn on its own line, the next above the last, so the way there and the way back never overlap.
_Avoid_: Row (that's an Inclined Plane's), leg, trip

On a Motion Map, every velocity arrow is on one scale, so equal speeds are drawn equal: the fastest is 0.8 of the widest gap between dots, or shorter if that would take an arrow up to the next dot ahead of it (as happens just before a stop). The **acceleration arrow** of a segment is one dashed arrow above its dots pointing the way the acceleration points, or a = 0. **Dots only** leaves off every velocity and acceleration arrow, for students to draw them. The **total displacement** is a solid arrow under the number line from where the motion starts to where it ends, labeled Δx; a motion that ends where it started has none. The letters at segment ends are the Motion Graph's. A motion map's arrows are short, so their heads are smaller than other figures'.

### Circuit Diagram

**Circuit Diagram**:
A schematic of a cell or battery with 1 to 4 resistors or bulbs, all in series or all in parallel, for current and resistance problems. Its generator is the Circuit Diagram Generator. It is drawn from a series/parallel tree built from its settings, not placed by hand (ADRs 0004 and 0005). Any other circuit is left to a Circuit Editor, planned but not built yet.
_Avoid_: Circuit (alone, for the figure), schematic (fine as a search word), wiring diagram

**Arrangement**:
How a Circuit Diagram's resistors and bulbs are joined: all in series round one loop, or all in parallel as the rungs of a ladder. A switch and an ammeter are always in the main line.
_Avoid_: Layout, topology, mode

**Part**:
One symbol on a circuit diagram: a battery, resistor, bulb, switch or ammeter. Each has a name label (R₁) and a value label (4 Ω). Names are numbered automatically until the teacher types one. On the Circuit Diagram a voltmeter is not a part: it goes across the battery or one part.
_Avoid_: Component, element, device

**Group**:
Parts joined in series or in parallel inside a circuit. A parallel group holds two or more branches.
_Avoid_: Block, cluster, sub-circuit

**Branch**:
A run of wire that carries one current: the main loop, or one branch of a parallel group. A current arrow belongs to a branch.
_Avoid_: Path, leg, rung (except for the drawing of a ladder)

**Point**:
A lettered dot on a wire (A, B…), for questions like "the potential difference between A and B". Points sit in the gaps between parts. The tree holds them, but the generator doesn't offer them for now.
_Avoid_: Node, junction (a junction is where wires meet, drawn as a dot with no letter), terminal

**Symbol style**:
US symbols (a zigzag resistor, a bulb with a looped filament) or IEC symbols, as UK GCSE uses (a box resistor, a bulb with a cross).
_Avoid_: Standard, notation

### Ray Diagram

**Ray Diagram**:
A figure of one lens or mirror on its principal axis, an object in front of it, the principal rays from the object's tip and the image they make, for optics. Its generator is the Ray Diagram Generator. It is drawn to scale in cm, the same across as up, and the image is exactly where the thin lens and mirror equation, 1/f = 1/d_o + 1/d_i, puts it. The light always comes from the left, so it has no Mirror (flip) switch: on this page a mirror is only ever the optical one.
_Avoid_: Optics diagram (fine as a search word), lens diagram

**Element**:
The one lens or mirror a Ray Diagram is of: a converging or diverging lens (drawn as its shape or as the line symbol), or a concave, convex or plane mirror (a shallow arc or a line, hatched on its back). Rays bend at the vertical line through a lens's middle, and reflect at the vertical line through a mirror's vertex, the thin-lens convention textbooks draw.
_Avoid_: Optic, device, part (that's a circuit's)

**Principal axis**:
The horizontal line through the middle of the element. The object stands on it, and the focal points are marked on it.
_Avoid_: Optical axis (fine as a search word), center line

**Focal point**:
A point marked on the principal axis with a dot and a label: F, F′, 2F and 2F′ on a lens (F and 2F on the object's side), F and C on a mirror (in front of a concave mirror, behind a convex one). A plane mirror has none.
_Avoid_: Focus (fine as a search word)

**Principal ray**:
One of the rays from the object's tip that a rule says where to draw: on a lens, parallel then through F′, through the center undeviated, and through F then parallel; on a mirror, parallel then through F, through F then parallel, through C and back on itself, and to the vertex and back at the same angle. A plane mirror has two rays from the tip, one to the mirror level with the object's foot and one above the tip. Each can be turned off, and each has small arrowheads showing which way the light goes. In color, each has its own color.
_Avoid_: Light ray (fine in captions), beam, line

On a Ray Diagram the **object** is always an arrow, upright or pointing down, standing on the principal axis, its distance and height set in cm.

**Image**:
The arrow where the principal rays from the object's tip meet. A real image is solid, where the rays themselves meet. A virtual image is dashed, where the rays' dashed extensions meet. The object at F of a converging lens or concave mirror has no image: its rays leave parallel. An image more than six focal lengths away is left off and the rays run off the figure's edge.
_Avoid_: Picture, reflection

**Shown**:
How much of a Ray Diagram is drawn: the setup (the element, principal axis, focal points and object, for students to draw the rays), the rays too, or everything, the rays and the image. All three are laid out alike, so a blank setup and its completed figure line up.
_Avoid_: Mode, stage, view

**Scale**:
A numbered cm ruler under a Ray Diagram, with 0 under the element, for students to measure distances.
_Avoid_: Ruler (fine as a search word), grid

The **answer key** gives the image distance and magnification, real is positive (an image distance is negative for a virtual image), with the words too: real or virtual, upright or inverted, magnified, diminished or the same size, and which side the image is on.

### Length Reading

**Length Reading**:
A figure of the instrument a length is read from: a ruler with an object lying along it, a caliper or a micrometer, picked by its instrument. Its generator is the Length Reading Generator, Physics' copy of the generator Math and Chemistry have too (Math's has only the ruler). The ruler is the default, and its figures are the same on all three sites.
_Avoid_: Measuring instruments generator, caliper generator

**Caliper**:
A 150 mm instrument for length, drawn with its outside jaws holding an object, and its inside jaws, depth rod, lock screw and thumb rest drawn but not used. It is read from its main scale and sliding scale to its least count. Its figure has a close-up strip of the scales below it.
_Avoid_: Vernier, vernier caliper, calliper, slide caliper (all fine in search keywords; the generator never says "vernier")

**Main scale**:
The caliper's fixed scale along its beam, marked every mm and numbered every cm (0, 1, 2…), as calipers print it.
_Avoid_: Beam scale

**Sliding scale**:
The short scale on the caliper's slider, below the main scale: 10 divisions over 9 mm, 20 over 39 mm or 50 over 49 mm, numbered 0 to 10 in tenths of a mm. Its zero sits at the reading, so exactly one of its marks lines up with a main-scale mark (at a whole mm, its zero and its last mark both do, as on a real caliper).
_Avoid_: Vernier, vernier scale (fine in search keywords)

**Least count**:
The smallest length an instrument reads: 0.1, 0.05 or 0.02 mm on the caliper, one main-scale division less one sliding-scale division, and 0.01 mm on the micrometer. It can be printed on the caliper's slider or the micrometer's frame, as real ones are.
_Avoid_: Precision (fine in help text), resolution

**Micrometer**:
A 0–25 mm instrument for length, drawn with its U-shaped frame and insulating pad, anvil, spindle, lock lever, sleeve, thimble and ratchet. It reads to 0.01 mm: the last sleeve mark showing, to the half mm, plus the thimble mark on the datum line. Read to one estimated digit, the default, it reads to 0.001 mm: how far the datum line is past that thimble mark, in tenths of a mark. Its figure has a round magnifier on the sleeve and thimble.
_Avoid_: Micrometer screw gauge, screw gauge (fine in search keywords)

**Sleeve**:
The micrometer's fixed barrel, with the datum line along it, whole mm marked above the line (numbered every 5) and half mm below it. A mark the thimble has only just uncovered is drawn a hair clear of its edge, so it plainly shows.
_Avoid_: Barrel, main scale (that's the caliper's)

**Thimble**:
The micrometer's turning sleeve, with 50 marks round its edge, 0.01 mm each, numbered every 5. Seen side on, its marks bunch toward its top and bottom edges, and the bigger numbers are above the datum line.
_Avoid_: Rotating scale, drum

**Datum line**:
The line along the micrometer's sleeve that the thimble's marks are read against.
_Avoid_: Reference line, index line

**Zero error**:
What a caliper or micrometer reads closed, with nothing in its jaws: positive or negative, up to 0.5 mm on the caliper and 0.05 mm on the micrometer. The jaws are that much closer together than the reading says, and the instrument can be drawn closed above the measuring one. The answer key gives the reading, the zero error and the corrected reading, the reading minus the zero error.
_Avoid_: Zero offset (that's a spring scale's), offset, calibration error

**Jaws**:
What holds the object on a caliper (its outside jaws) or a micrometer (its anvil and spindle): nothing, a block, a cylinder, a ball or a wire, drawn as wide as the reading less the zero error.
_Avoid_: Clamp, grip

### Lever

**Lever**:
A figure of a level, weightless beam resting on a fulcrum, with one load and one effort on it, always balanced. Its generator is the Lever Generator. The beam is drawn to scale from its two arms, and the effort is worked out from the load and the arms. A beam with several weights, its own weight, two supports or no balance is a Torque figure, not a Lever.
_Avoid_: Seesaw (fine as a search word), simple machine (fine as a search word), balance

**Lever class**:
Which part is in the middle of a lever: the fulcrum (class 1, both forces down), the load (class 2, the effort up and farther out) or the effort (class 3, the effort up and nearer the fulcrum). Arms that don't suit the class are swapped.
_Avoid_: Type, kind, order (as in "first-order lever")

**Fulcrum**:
The point a lever turns about, drawn as a triangle under the beam standing on a hatched ground line. On a class 2 or 3 lever it sits a little in from one end of the beam. Lever figures say "fulcrum"; "pivot" is the Torque figure's word.
_Avoid_: Pivot (on a Lever figure), support, hinge

**Effort**:
The force put on a lever to work it, an arrow down onto the beam on a class 1 lever and up from it on class 2 and 3. It is never typed: it is the load's weight × the load arm ÷ the effort arm, in full when it comes out even and otherwise to 3 significant figures, trailing zeros kept (0.0300 N), in lb for a load in lb and in N otherwise.
_Avoid_: Input force (fine as a search word), applied force, push

**Load**:
What a lever moves: a force in N or lb, drawn as a block (an Object) on the beam or an arrow down onto it, or a mass in kg or g, always drawn as a block, whose weight is its mass × 9.8 N/kg, rounded like the effort.
_Avoid_: Resistance, output force (both fine as search words), weight (for the thing drawn)

**Effort arm**:
The distance from the fulcrum to the effort, along the beam, marked with a dimension line under the ground.
_Avoid_: Effort distance, input arm, moment arm

**Load arm**:
The distance from the fulcrum to the load, along the beam, marked with a dimension line under the ground. No arm is drawn shorter than 15% of the beam, so its label fits; the form says when an arm isn't to scale.
_Avoid_: Load distance, resistance arm, output arm

**Mechanical advantage**:
The effort arm ÷ the load arm, which for a balanced lever is also the load ÷ the effort. It is in the answer key, never on the figure.
_Avoid_: MA (fine in the answer key), leverage, ratio

### Circular Motion

**Circular Motion**:
A figure of an object going round a circle at a steady speed, in one of three setups, with its velocity along the tangent, its centripetal acceleration toward the center, and the forces on it. Its generator is the Circular Motion Generator. A planet or moon going round is a future orbits generator's; a track of several hills and dips is a Roller Coaster figure.
_Avoid_: Centrifugal (never drawn or named), orbit, UCM diagram (UCM is fine as a search word)

**Setup**:
Which of the three ways the Circular Motion figure draws the circle: the Top view, the Vertical circle or the Conical pendulum. Each has a choice or two of its own, and fixes which forces can be drawn; they are only switched on and named.
_Avoid_: View (alone), mode, scenario

**Top view**:
The circle seen from above, dashed, with its center, and the object at 1 to 8 positions on it: on its own, on a string to the center, on a turntable (with a second object halfway out, if wanted) or on a flat road. It shows velocity, acceleration and one inward force, never gravity. With a second object, the first one's radius is measured beside the line through both, from the center all the way out.
_Avoid_: Horizontal circle, overhead view

**Vertical circle**:
The circle standing upright, seen from the side, with the object at the top, side or bottom: on a string, inside a loop, at the top of a hill or the bottom of a dip. Gravity is one length everywhere; the tension or normal force is shorter than gravity at the top and longer at the bottom, so the net force points to the center. The speed is steady, so the net force is the same everywhere: at the bottom the tension is two gravities longer than at the top, and at the side it is the whole net force. Over a hill the normal force is less than gravity, and a dip mirrors it.
_Avoid_: Loop-the-loop (it's a loop), side view (alone)

**Conical pendulum**:
A bob on a string from a hatched ceiling at 15° to 60° from the vertical, going round a level circle drawn as an ellipse, its back half lighter. Its forces are to scale: the tension's vertical component is as long as gravity. At a small angle the horizontal component is too short to come out from under the bob, so it is drawn from the vertical component's tip to the tension's. Its velocity is along the line of sight, a dot (out of the page) or a cross (into it).
_Avoid_: Cone pendulum, swing

**Inward force**:
The Top view's one solid arrow toward the center, beside the acceleration, named by the teacher for what supplies it (F_c, T, F_f, F_g). The side views draw the real forces instead.
_Avoid_: Centripetal force (fine as its default label and as a search word), net force, center-seeking force

**Position**:
One of the places the object is drawn on the circle, all evenly spaced from the first, which can be lettered A, B, C… in the order the object reaches them. Past four, only the first position's vectors are labeled.
_Avoid_: Point (fine in "top and bottom points"), instant, snapshot

### Roller Coaster

**Roller Coaster**:
A picture of a track of hills and dips, and maybe one loop, with an object on it, labelled points and their heights, for conservation of energy. Its generator is the Roller Coaster Generator. Only the heights and the loop's radius are to scale; nothing (speed, energy) is worked out from them. A single hill or dip with its radius and forces is a Circular Motion figure.
_Avoid_: Energy track (fine as a search word), graph (it's a picture, not a graph)

**Track**:
The smooth curve the object rides, through the points from left to right. It never goes higher than a hill's height or lower than a dip's, and two points at the same height make a level stretch.
_Avoid_: Path, rail, curve (alone)

**Point**:
One row of the track: a place on it, evenly spaced from the others, with a height from 0 to 100. A point higher than both neighbours is the top of a hill, a lower one the bottom of a dip. It can have a dot and a label (A, PE_g, KE = 0) or none, when it only shapes the track.
_Avoid_: Node, waypoint, knot

**Hill** and **dip**:
A point higher, or lower, than both its neighbours, where the track is level.
_Avoid_: Bump (for a hill), valley (for a dip; fine as a search word)

**Height mark**:
A dimension line from the ground straight up to a point, with its label beside it (40 m, h, h/2) or a line to write on. Point heights are measured from the ground line.
_Avoid_: Height arrow, altitude

**Loop**:
A full circle standing on one point, at its bottom, where the track is level. Its radius is in the units of the heights and drawn to scale with them, unless there's no room, when it's drawn smaller and the panel says so. One per figure.
_Avoid_: Loop-de-loop and loop-the-loop (fine as search words), circle (alone)

**Rough stretch**:
The track from one point to the next with friction on it, hatched along its underside. Its length mark is horizontal, under the ground: on a level stretch its length, on a slope its distance along the ground. A rough stretch with no length label of its own is taken into the mark of the one before it.
_Avoid_: Friction zone, rough patch

### Torque

**Torque**:
A figure of a level beam held up by a Support, with up to six Loads at set distances along it, for torque and the principle of moments. Its generator is the Torque Generator, which works out the torques, the Support forces or the cable's tension beside the figure. The beam is always drawn level, balanced or not. A beam with one load and one effort, named by its lever class, is a Lever figure; a ladder against a wall is neither, for now.
_Avoid_: Seesaw, balance and beam balance (fine as search words), moment diagram (moment is fine in About copy and search words; the page says torque)

**Beam**:
The bar the loads are on, drawn to scale along its length (up to 480 px), light or with its own weight drawn at its middle. Places along it are measured from its left end, which is the wall's end; mirrored, from its right end.
_Avoid_: Rod, plank (fine in an example), lever

**Support**:
What holds a Torque figure's beam up: one Pivot, two supports A and B (triangles like the Pivot), or a hatched wall at the beam's end, with a Cable (a hinge dot where the beam meets the wall) or without one (the beam fixed into the wall, a cantilever).
_Avoid_: Setup, mount, fulcrum

**Pivot**:
The one triangle a Torque figure's beam rests on, anywhere along it. Torques are taken about it. The Lever's word for the same triangle is fulcrum.
_Avoid_: Fulcrum (on a Torque figure; fine as a search word), axis, hinge

**Load**:
One thing on a Torque figure's beam: an Object hanging from it on a string, an Object resting on it, or a force arrow down or up with no object. Its value is a weight in N or a mass in kg or g (all the same unit), and its label shows the value unless the teacher types over it.
_Avoid_: Mass or weight (for the thing drawn), effort (that's a Lever's)

**Distance mark**:
A dimension line from the Pivot, support A or the wall to a load (and to B, or to where the Cable meets the beam), stacked in rows above the beam, the nearest load on the lowest row, with dashed lines down to what it measures. Rows that don't fit are left off, farthest first, and the form says so.
_Avoid_: Lever arm, moment arm (fine as search words), dimension

**Cable**:
The line from a beam up to the wall it is hinged to, at 15° to 75° to the beam, with an angle mark at the beam. It is drawn at its true angle, so a steep cable shortens the beam drawn; too steep to fit, it meets the wall above the figure.
_Avoid_: Rope, wire, string (a string holds a hanging object)

**Support force**:
A force holding the beam up, drawn as a solid arrow: up at the Pivot or at A and B (down onto the beam when it comes out down: a pivot under forces that are all up, or a support the beam would lift off), or at a wall its parts along the beam and up the wall, with the Cable's tension along the cable. A beam fixed into a wall also gets the wall's moment, a curved arrow labelled M round the beam's end, turning against the loads (on the other side of the beam from the wall's force). Off by default, since they are often the answer.
_Avoid_: Reaction (fine as a search word), normal force

### Pendulum

**Pendulum**:
A figure of a simple pendulum: a Bob on a string from a Pivot under a hatched ceiling, swung out 5° to 75° from the vertical, either as the Swing or as one bob with the forces on it. Its generator is the Pendulum Generator. The string is one length in every figure, so θ alone sets the shape; nothing is worked out from it. A bob going round a level circle is the Circular Motion figure's conical pendulum, and a spring is the Spring Generator's.
_Avoid_: Swing (for the figure; that's one picture of it), simple harmonic oscillator (fine as a search word)

**Pivot**:
The dot under the ceiling the string hangs from, and the point θ is measured at. The Torque figure uses the word for its beam's triangle.
_Avoid_: Hinge, support, suspension point

**Bob**:
The ball at the end of a pendulum's string. Its label (m) is its own.
_Avoid_: Weight (that's the force on it), mass (fine in its label), ball, object (alone)

The pendulum's **string** is drawn solid from the pivot to the bob, never a rope or rod, with its length label (L) beside it.

**Swing**:
The picture of the bob at three places, for energy problems: its release point, the bottom, and the far side, at the same height as the release point, with its dashed path between them. The bottom and far-side bobs are lighter copies with dashed strings. It needs θ of at least 15°, so the three bobs stand apart, and it can letter them A, B, C and show the velocity at the bottom (none at the two ends, where the bob is at rest).
_Avoid_: Three positions, energy diagram, oscillation (fine as a search word)

**Release point**:
Where the bob is let go from rest, θ from the vertical on the chosen side: the solid bob in the Swing, and the one bob otherwise. Forces are drawn only there, on one bob: weight straight down and the tension as long as weight's component along the string, mg cos θ, which is right while the bob is at rest; weight's components (mg cos θ along the string, mg sin θ along the path) are thinner and dashed, as on the Free Body Diagram. All are to scale with each other, weight drawn longer at a wide angle so mg cos θ shows past the bob, except mg sin θ at a small angle, drawn just long enough to show.
_Avoid_: Start, amplitude position, extreme

**Height mark**:
On a Pendulum, a dimension line on the far side from the release point, from the release point's height down to the lowest point's, with dashed lines across to them, labelled h. When it is too short for its arrowheads, they sit outside it, pointing in. The Roller Coaster's height mark is measured from the ground instead.
_Avoid_: Height arrow, drop

### Spring

**Spring**:
A figure of a spring, or two or three joined in series or in parallel, fixed to a Support with an Object on its free end, drawn once, Before and after, or at Three positions, for Hooke's law and simple harmonic motion. Its generator is the Spring Generator. Labels are text, so nothing is worked out or drawn to scale, and springs in series stretch equally whatever their k. Reading a spring scale is the Spring Scale's; a spring inside a ramp or pulley setup is left to those generators.
_Avoid_: Slinky, coil (alone, for the figure), spring diagram (fine as a search word)

**Support**:
What a Spring figure's springs are fixed to: a hatched ceiling they hang from, or a hatched wall on the left with a hatched floor the Object rests on. Hanging copies share one ceiling; horizontal copies are rows, each with its own wall and floor.
_Avoid_: Mount, anchor, base

**Natural length**:
How long a spring is with nothing pulling or pushing it, L₀. Drawn as a dashed line where the free end would be, and as a dimension line on the spring drawn at it.
_Avoid_: Rest length, unstretched length (fine in captions), original length

**Extension**:
How far the free end has moved from where it would be at the natural length, as a share of that length: up to 100% stretched, or, on a floor, down to −50%, squashed. Marked with a dimension line labelled x. A hanging spring with an Object on it is always stretched, by at least 5%.
_Avoid_: Stretch (as a noun), displacement (fine for Three positions), compression (fine for a squashed spring)

**Equilibrium line**:
A dashed line across the figure where the free end rests: on a floor, at the natural length; hanging, where the Object hangs at rest, stretched, so it is left off while an applied force pulls a hanging Object further. Labelled x = 0 unless the teacher types another label. The Wave's rest line is a different thing.
_Avoid_: Rest line (that's a Wave's), midline, zero line

**Before and after**:
The Spring figure drawn twice: at the natural length (hanging, with nothing on it but a hook), then stretched or squashed with the Object on it, side by side under one ceiling or in two rows, with the natural length's dashed line through both so the extension reads off.
_Avoid_: Comparison, two states

**Three positions**:
The Spring figure drawn at −A, at equilibrium and at +A, for simple harmonic motion: side by side when hanging (+A, up, on the left) or three rows on a floor (−A, squashed, on top). A is the amplitude. A hanging spring oscillates about where it hangs at rest, stretched by half its natural length. Only one spring, never series or parallel. At each end the acceleration arrow points back to equilibrium and there is no velocity arrow; at equilibrium there is only the velocity arrow, toward or away from the Support.
_Avoid_: Snapshots, SHM positions, phases

**Coil style**:
How a spring's coil is drawn: a zigzag or loops (a helix seen from the side). Either is redrawn at every length with the same number of turns and the same line weight, never scaled, so a stretched spring looks like the same spring pulled out. Squashed loops are drawn narrower along the spring so they never cross.
_Avoid_: Spring type, coil shape
